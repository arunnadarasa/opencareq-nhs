Seminar Abstract & Speaker Biography

Abstract

Managing multimorbid long-term conditions—such as severe asthma, type 2 diabetes, cardio-kidney-metabolic (CKM) disease, and polypharmacy—across large public health systems is rarely a static single-disease classification problem. Across England's 42 NHS Integrated Care Boards (1,280 Primary Care Networks; 62.93M population), patients move through cyclical clinical-social feedback loops coupled with intracellular drug-depot memory and tight neighbourhood clinic capacity.

This talk introduces OpenCareQ, an open, non-commercial, gate-free Python healthcare quantum DSL governed by an adversarial Gate G4 classical-first compiler (routing 95% of tractable scheduling and low-degree homology in < 20 ms to exact SciPy HiGHS MILP, PT-MPO, and sparse Hodge SVD, while compiling frustrated 3-body PCN HUBOs, Non-Markovian Quantum Combs, and higher-order QTDA Dirac kernels to Quantinuum Guppy/HUGR, CUDA-Q, Haiqu, and HQS open-system models across 193/193 zero-fit benchmarks).

Speaker Bio — Arun Nadarasa

Arun Nadarasa is a Clinical Pharmacist and NHS Innovation Fellow at Mid and South Essex NHS Foundation Trust, and a leader within the International Social Prescribing Pharmacy Association (ISPPA). He is the Founder of the Association for Clinical Quantum, an independent think tank helping clinicians and health systems evaluate quantum technologies responsibly across primary, community, and secondary care, and Founder of the Clinical AI & Quantum Hackathon.

He created OpenCareQ (stewarded non-commercially via INALYN CIC #17398798) and was a 2nd Place winner (Open Innovation Track) at the Quantinuum Bradford Hackathon 2025.
